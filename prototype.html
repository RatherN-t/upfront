Auto-spread — on by default

Spreading across 25 books cuts the worst-case outcome from −34.05% to −8.71%. It does not raise your return — the mean stays around 10.8% either way — and it makes small losses more frequent while making catastrophic ones rare. Past 25 books it stops helping, because correlation means you cannot diversify away the economy.

Before your first investment

Three questions

ASIC suggests marketplace lenders check investor understanding before a first investment. We agree with them.

1. If the business you funded goes insolvent, what happens to your capital?

2. Can you withdraw before the book finishes collecting?

3. Is the expected return guaranteed?

All three correct. You can invest.
Under the hood

How a book gets priced.

An invoice and a subscription are not the same asset. An invoice is money owed for work already delivered — the debtor owes it whether or not the supplier survives. A subscription is money owed for service not yet delivered. If the business fails, the service stops and the stream stops with it. That one distinction changes loss severity by more than 3×.

POST /payments · soft retries only, with Nonce

Soft codes retry. Hard codes never do — retrying a blocked-by-bank burns a $5 decline fee and cannot succeed. Every handler is idempotent on the evt_ id, because a payment can settle then reverse days later.

Transfers arrive net of dishonours and fees. We reconcile investors against net settled cash, which is why a failed customer payment can never silently reach them.

Our first model said 14.25% and it was wrong. Five errors, all pointing the same direction. This is what changed.
1
Pinch fees weren't in the model at all
$4,683 on Voltride's book — about 63% of the entire gross margin, simply absent.
2
Ticket size and cadence were treated as cosmetic
Same revenue, weekly vs quarterly billing, is a 4.4× difference in cost. This inverted which books we thought were good.
3
Subscription revenue was modelled as a receivable
It isn't. Service not yet delivered dies with the business. Continuation is 15%, not 100%.
4
Default probability came from the wrong population
We used the economy-wide base rate. Prospa, Australia's largest listed SME lender, realises 5.7–9.9% net bad debt at a 35% yield.
5
A mean was reported with no distribution behind it
SME defaults are correlated. The recession that closes the gym closes the tutoring studio the same quarter.
Corrected

Mean 14.72%. Median 18.65%. 5th percentile 2.49%. 1st percentile −22.46%. Probability of loss 4.00%. Probability of underperforming a term deposit 6.44%.

The mean was roughly right. The framing was not. A mid-teens return in small-business credit is a correctly priced risk, not a safe one, and it has to be shown as a distribution.
